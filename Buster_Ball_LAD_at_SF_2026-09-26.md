# BUSTER BALL PODCAST
## September 26, 2026

Giants fans, let’s make the Dodgers work for their weekend. Two games left, a chance to spoil their push for a hundred wins, and an afternoon to find out whether Matt Wilkinson can make the right adjustments.

I’m Billy B. First pitch at Oracle Park is 1:05 Pacific, with Wilkinson and Blake Snell listed as the probable starters.

## Last Night

Friday’s opener finished Dodgers two, Giants nothing. Tarik Skubal delivered seven scoreless innings and struck out ten. Teoscar Hernández and Andy Pages supplied solo homers. That’s a tough combination to overcome.

My takeaway isn’t that the Giants need to swing harder today. They need to give themselves better pitches to hit. Build an inning before trying to change the entire afternoon with one swing.

## The Matchup

Here’s where it gets interesting. Wilkinson threw five scoreless innings against these Dodgers on September 20, striking out six. Now comes the rematch.

He brings a 3.65 ERA, but we’re talking about only twenty-four and two-thirds big-league innings. The encouraging part is four walks against eighteen strikeouts. His strikeout-minus-walk rate works out to about fourteen percent: useful control, not overwhelming dominance.

His fastball averages about ninety miles an hour, and his overall whiff rate is just under nineteen percent. My read: don’t ask him to overpower this lineup. Ask him to change speeds, hit his targets and keep the defense involved.

I’d establish the fastball without living in the middle, then make the changeup part of the conversation early. Don’t wait until a hitter has seen three straight fastballs before showing something different.

Across the way, Snell’s season has produced a 1.86 ERA. More revealing for today’s approach: hitters have chased roughly forty percent of his pitches outside the zone, and missed on about thirty-six percent of their swings. Those are two different measures pointing toward the same problem—he gets hitters to help him.

He’s also scheduled to return after left groin tightness. I haven’t verified a pitch limit, so I’m not putting a number on his workload.

So what does that mean this afternoon? Be selective, not passive. My plan would be to look for a fastball in one manageable area before two strikes. If it’s there, attack it. If it isn’t, don’t let the breaking ball below the zone turn a favorable count into his count.

## The Bats

I don’t have a verified starting lineup for this recording. Among the available Giants hitters, Victor Bericoto, Bo Davidson and Drew Gilbert are names I’m watching, depending on who gets the assignment.

For Bericoto, my assignment is simple: hunt something he can drive without expanding the target. With Davidson, I want to see the quality of the decisions, not just whether a ball drops. If Gilbert gets an opportunity, the same rule applies—keep the inning moving rather than trying to rescue it alone.

The injury picture matters. Rafael Devers, Jung Hoo Lee and Bryce Eldridge remain sidelined; Osleivis Basabe is listed day to day with hamstring tightness.

On the other side, Hernández and Pages are the immediate reminders that the dangerous at-bat isn’t always the one you circled before the game. My pitching plan would be to avoid predictable get-me-over strikes after retiring the headline hitters. Make every part of the order earn its baserunners.

## The Bullpen

Now, about protecting a close game. Sam Hentges, Joel Kuhnel, Drew Smith and Tristan Beck each pitched a scoreless inning Friday, throwing between ten and thirteen pitches apiece.

That’s light work by pitch count, but it isn’t a guarantee that all four are available again. I haven’t verified today’s availability decisions.

Camilo Doval is back after a waiver claim, but he was unavailable Friday. His readiness today remains unconfirmed.

I wouldn’t automatically hand him the biggest moment just because the name is familiar. Confirm who’s ready, then choose the matchup.

For Wilkinson, I’d prepare a reliever as the lineup approaches its third look. That isn’t an automatic hook. If his location holds and the contact stays manageable, let him continue. But if the fastball starts drifting into the middle, don’t wait for the scoreboard to confirm what your eyes already told you.

And if the biggest threat arrives in the sixth, that’s the leverage inning. Use the best available matchup then. A clean ninth won’t help if the game gets away earlier.

## Scout’s Notebook

My notebook has three checks today: Can Wilkinson change his sequence the second time through? Can Giants hitters recognize Snell’s off-speed pitches before committing? And can the defense turn routine contact into routine outs?

Those are evaluation questions, not predictions disguised as statistics. I’m much more interested in repeatable decisions than declaring a breakthrough from one afternoon.

## Three Keys

First, win the chase battle. Make Snell bring the pitch into the hitting area, then be ready when he does.

Second, keep Wilkinson out of rescue mode. I’d prioritize strikes with movement and changing speeds over nibbling for a perfect pitch.

Third, manage the inning that’s actually in front of you. Don’t spend an out chasing an uncertain extra base, and don’t save a ready reliever for a situation that may never arrive.

## Road to 100

Here’s the math from today’s schedule context. At sixty-five wins and ninety-five losses, the Giants can finish with sixty-seven wins, and they can’t reach a hundred losses.

The Dodgers are ninety-eight and sixty-two. They need both remaining games to reach a hundred wins. One Giants victory shuts that door.

That’s a modest consolation, sure. I’d still take it.

## The Pick

I’m picking Dodgers four, Giants two. My concern is whether San Francisco can create enough good swings before the game reaches the late innings.

The Giants’ route to an upset is clear in my mind: disciplined at-bats, another controlled outing from Wilkinson, and a bullpen decision made one batter early rather than one batter late. Give me that kind of game, and I’ll happily be wrong about the score.
