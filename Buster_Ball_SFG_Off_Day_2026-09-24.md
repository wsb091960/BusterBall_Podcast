# BUSTER BALL PODCAST
## September 24, 2026

Giants fans, there’s no first pitch today—but there’s a chance tomorrow to make the Dodgers’ weekend uncomfortable. That’s worth getting ready for.

I’m Billy B. This is the Buster Ball Podcast, and we’re using this Thursday off-day to reset before Los Angeles comes to Oracle Park on Friday, September 25, at 7:15 Pacific.

## Yesterday’s Takeaway

Wednesday’s finale went to Minnesota, four to two. Cesar Perdomo gave the Giants five and a third innings, allowed two runs, and didn’t walk anybody. Shay Whitcomb supplied both San Francisco runs with a fourth-inning single, but the Twins broke the tie with two in the seventh. Five Giants hits just weren’t enough.

My takeaway? Don’t confuse a competitive start with a complete winning formula. I liked the absence of free passes from Perdomo. Now I want the offense to give that kind of outing some breathing room.

## The Dodgers Test

Yunior Marte is listed as San Francisco’s probable starter Friday. The Dodgers’ starter remains unannounced in the schedule information I could verify, so any pitcher-specific hitting plan has to stay provisional. We’re not filling in that blank with a guess.

Here’s where it gets interesting: Marte just faced this club on September 19. He allowed six runs in four and two-thirds innings, with three walks and two strikeouts. His early major-league sample shows a strikeout-minus-walk rate of only about two and a half percentage points. That’s a narrow margin for escaping trouble.

The pitch-level clue is his four-seamer. It’s averaged about ninety-three, but generated misses on only seven percent of swings. The splitter has produced a thirty-three percent whiff rate—promising, but across just forty pitches. That’s something to explore, not something to declare solved.

My plan would be to establish a strike without becoming predictable. Show the secondary pitches before two strikes. If Marte can land one early, he doesn’t have to spend every difficult count trying to squeeze a fastball past somebody.

And I wouldn’t promise him a third trip through the lineup. Watch the swings, the location and the traffic. If those start moving in the wrong direction, get help ready before the inning gets away.

## The Bats to Watch

Shohei Ohtani returned from the injured list September 23. Don’t build Friday’s plan around his absence.

Against him, I’d rather change eye levels and speeds than keep trying to expand farther and farther off the plate. Make a competitive pitch. Don’t turn caution into an automatic baserunner.

For San Francisco, Bo Davidson is a player I want to watch closely. He’s opened his major-league career with three hits in ten at-bats across three games. Nice beginning. Much too early for a grand conclusion.

I’m watching whether he recognizes the adjustment: can he leave the tempting pitch alone and still attack the next strike? That tells me more this weekend than whether one ground ball sneaks through.

The injury picture matters, too. Jung Hoo Lee and Bryce Eldridge are out for the season; Osleivis Basabe remains day to day with hamstring tightness.

Until the opposing starter and the lineups are confirmed, I’d keep the offensive message simple: hunt a hittable strike, not merely a fastball. Patience doesn’t mean watching your best pitch go by.

## Bullpen Plan

Now, about protecting a close game. The Giants used five relievers Wednesday. Roxby threw twenty-eight pitches, Kuhnel nineteen, Dylan Smith seventeen, Hentges eleven and Harris seven. Today provides a recovery day, but I haven’t verified individual clearance or a complete recent-workload assessment for Friday. Nobody gets an automatic “fully rested” stamp.

My preference is to have both a length option and a matchup option ready behind Marte. Those are different jobs.

If the dangerous part of the order comes up with two aboard in the sixth, use the best available matchup then. I’m not saving my strongest available arm for a ninth-inning lead that might never arrive.

And if Marte is cruising? Let him earn another hitter. Don’t remove him just because the lineup card has turned over—but don’t let a tidy pitch count overrule deteriorating command, either.

## Scout’s Notebook

Three keys for Friday.

First, **make the secondary pitches part of the count-building plan**. I want Marte creating uncertainty, not announcing fastball every time he needs a strike.

Second, **protect every out**. Take the sure play defensively. On the bases, be aggressive when the read is clean, not because the offense feels overdue.

Third, **win the first big decision**. That might be a pitching change in the fifth or a pinch-hit opportunity in the seventh. Manage the situation in front of you, not the inning you hoped to reach.

My notebook question is simple: can the Giants force Los Angeles to make the next adjustment?
