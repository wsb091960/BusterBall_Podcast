# BUSTER BALL PODCAST
## Friday, September 25, 2026

Giants fans, spoiling the Dodgers’ weekend won’t fix this season. But it would make these final few nights a whole lot sweeter.

I’m Billy B. Tonight, it’s Dodgers–Giants at Oracle Park, first pitch at 7:15 Pacific, with Tarik Skubal and Yunior Marte the probable starters.

San Francisco comes in at 65 and 94; Los Angeles is 97 and 62. That’s the backdrop. My question is simpler: can the Giants keep this close enough to make one good swing matter late?

## Last Time Out

Wednesday’s finale against Minnesota was there for the taking. Cesar Perdomo allowed two runs over five and a third innings without walking anybody. Shay Whitcomb supplied both Giants runs with a fourth-inning single, but Minnesota broke the tie in the seventh and won, four to two. San Francisco finished with five hits.

That’s the lesson I’d carry into tonight: a competitive start needs some help. Don’t ask the pitching staff to navigate every inning with absolutely no room for error.

## The Matchup

Here’s where it gets interesting. Skubal’s season isn’t just a shiny ERA. He’s striking out 29.5 percent of hitters and walking only 4.5 percent—a 25-point strikeout-minus-walk gap. His expected weighted on-base average allowed, which accounts for contact quality along with strikeouts and walks, is just .271. He’s earning those results.

So what does that mean tonight? Patience can’t become passivity. My approach would be to pick a hittable fastball early and commit to it, rather than take two strikes hoping a walk appears.

The pitch I’d be most careful with is his changeup. It’s producing misses on nearly 47 percent of swings this season. That’s a strong argument for making him bring it up instead of helping him below the zone.

Whitcomb is one bat I’m watching after his two-hit Wednesday. Bo Davidson also singled and scored in that game. Neither gets an automatic spot in tonight’s lineup—I haven’t verified a posted batting order—but those are two hitters I’d watch for disciplined swings rather than trying to manufacture a highlight.

On the other side, Marte has only nineteen and a third major-league innings behind him. Eleven strikeouts, nine walks. That’s a tiny sample, but it makes strike-throwing my first checkpoint. His scouting report identifies a fastball with carry and arm-side movement, a slider for righties, and a splitter that’s important against lefties.

My plan for Marte: establish a strike before asking hitters to chase. Against lefties, show the splitter early enough that they can’t simply wait for a fastball. Against righties, change their eye level and keep the slider out of the middle.

Shohei Ohtani and Andy Pages have returned from injuries, so I wouldn’t build this plan around a weakened Dodgers lineup.

If Ohtani starts, I want Marte changing speeds without conceding the count. With Pages, I’d prioritize location over trying to overpower him. Those are my matchup priorities, not claims of a verified weakness.

## Bullpen Decisions

Now, the game inside the game: how long do you stay with Marte?

I’d assess him closely as the order turns over a second time. Are the misses competitive? Is the splitter getting believable swings? If he reaches a third trip, he needs to have earned it with command—not just a manageable pitch count. That’s my recommendation, not an announced innings limit.

Wednesday required five Giants relievers: Joel Kuhnel, Dylan Smith, Sam Hentges, Braxton Roxby and Trent Harris. Roxby worked an inning and two-thirds; Harris recorded one out.

Thursday’s scheduled break provides a recovery opportunity, but I haven’t verified individual pitch totals or tonight’s availability declarations. I’m not calling everybody fully rested.

The roster headline: San Francisco claimed Camilo Doval from Pittsburgh on September 24. I haven’t confirmed his availability tonight.

And the injury picture still matters: Jung Hoo Lee is on the injured list with a groin contusion; Bryce Eldridge with a concussion.

Blade Tidwell moved to the 60-day injured list Thursday with a right UCL sprain.

My leverage rule tonight is straightforward: use the best available matchup when the game is actually on the line. If that’s the sixth inning with runners aboard, don’t save your strongest option for a ninth inning that may never become relevant. And if Doval is available, I wouldn’t hand him the biggest assignment solely because the name is familiar.

## Three Tactical Keys

First, attack a pitch, not a reputation. Against Skubal, I want selective aggression on something the hitter can drive—not defensive swings from the first pitch onward.

Second, make Los Angeles earn its baserunners. For Marte, I’d rather see a well-located strike put in play than a sequence of noncompetitive pitches followed by a forced fastball.

Third, manage before the inning gets away. Have the next pitcher ready when Marte’s command starts slipping. Don’t wait for the scoreboard to confirm what the last three hitters already showed you.

## Scout’s Notebook

My notebook has three questions tonight. Can the young hitters recognize the changeup without slowing their bats against the fastball? Can Marte repeat his delivery with runners on? And can the defense turn a routine chance into a routine out when the pressure rises?

Those answers would tell me more than one loud foul ball or one impressive radar-gun reading.

## Road to 100

Here’s the arithmetic with three games remaining: the Giants can finish with no more than 68 wins or 97 losses. Neither 100 wins nor 100 losses is in play. The Dodgers, at 97 wins, need a sweep to reach 100. One Giants victory closes that door.

That’s a modest target. I’d still enjoy watching them hit it.

## The Pick

My prediction is Dodgers five, Giants two. I give Los Angeles the edge in the starting matchup, and I’m not counting on enough Giants offense to reverse it.

The upset path, though, is clear: cash in an early mistake, keep the walks down, and turn this into a late matchup game rather than a long night of catch-up.
